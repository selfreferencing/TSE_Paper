### (1) Do the four cohere as one paper's formal spine?

**Yes — three of them do, tightly. The fourth is a corollary, not a co-equal.**

`RosterSchema`, `StakeMedian`, and `SupportPriority` form a complete argument of the classical
shape: a general impossibility, a sharp instance showing the classical escape route fails, and a
classification of what survives. The instance is what makes it publishable rather than merely
formal — **median voting on single-peaked preferences is the canonical Gibbard–Satterthwaite
escape, and `median_spawn_manipulable` kills it under replication with a three-line
counterexample** (peaks {0,5,10}, the agent at 0 mints two identities at its own peak, the median
moves to 0). Then `measure_rule_split_invariant` draws the exact boundary: any rule reading a
profile only through its stake measure is split-invariant. Impossibility, its sharpest instance,
and the precise possibility frontier. That is a paper.

`JuryInversion` is different in kind. It is an **epistemic corollary** — under replication the
effective sample is the lineage count, `nEff = n²/∑kᵢ²`, and `nEff_le_lineages` by Cauchy–Schwarz,
so cloning adds heads but never information. It is a fine result and it belongs in the same paper,
but as a short final section drawing out a consequence, **not as a fourth pillar**. Presenting it
co-equally would blur what the paper is about: it answers "what does replication do to what a
jury *knows*", where the other three answer "what does replication do to what a rule can *do*".
Keep it, subordinate it.

**Agreed on `AxiologyExploits`.** Different object, different literature, and its own referee
caveat is correct that objective-level vulnerabilities are weaker in kind than the mechanism-design
results. Separating it is right.

### (3) The ambiguity — my view, with the caveat that it is a view

Your §4 asks whether the impossibility paper is the endogenous-electorate draft raised to full
generality or a sibling. **I now have both objects** — your addendum was right that I can read the
archive — so here is a straight answer: **one paper, and it is the draft generalized.**

The two facts you cite point the same way rather than opposite ways.
`measure_rule_split_invariant` is not merely *like* the draft's stake-weighted positive result; it
is that result with the Condorcet scaffolding removed, and the draft's Theorem on stake-weighted
rules becomes a corollary of it. `roster_impossibility` stands to the draft's one-sybil
impossibility the same way: the draft proves it for Condorcet-winner consistency, the module
proves it for any anonymous, spawn-monotone, non-constant binary rule. **Both halves of the draft
— impossibility and possibility — are special cases of the module results.** A paper whose two
main theorems are both corollaries of another paper's two main theorems is not a sibling; it is a
draft of it.

What the draft has that the modules do not is the *framing*: the game-theoretic derivation of
population-stability as a Nash condition, the signed-opposite relation to participation, and the
literature placement. That is the part worth keeping verbatim. The formal spine should be replaced
wholesale by the modules.

**One caution against the merge, in fairness to the other reading.** `SupportPriority` classifies
clone-proof rules over *arbitrary alternative types with finiteness not assumed*, which is a
strictly larger setting than the draft's. If the classification is the centrepiece, that argues
for a separate, more abstract paper and the draft stays as the applied one. My read is that the
classification is strongest as the *answer* to the impossibility rather than as a standalone —
but that is a judgement about what makes the better paper, not about the mathematics, and the
incoming collaborator may reasonably see it the other way. Your ruling correctly marks this
provisional; I would not overturn that on my read alone.

Why that matters now specifically. v4 did not weaken the endogenous-electorate result; it
rebuilt it on Overwhelming-Bloc and made it *stronger*. But the theorem — no rule is immune to an
agent manufacturing copies of itself — is the central object of an existing literature:

independence of clones (Tideman 1987), and current sybil-resilient social choice. A referee in
economics or computer science will place it there in the first minute. Uncited, it reads as
unaware; cited, the paper commands the literature instead.

**The fix is nearly free, because Kevin has already written it.** The standalone draft
`EEI4/endogenous_electorate_v4.tex` in the `Agentic-Capital-Desktop` archive engages exactly this
material — sybil-resilient social choice, electoral control by adding voters, and
population-stability related to Moulin's participation axiom as its "signed opposite." Porting
that related-work paragraph into TSE's §16 closes the gap without new research.

**Separately, `Rasch and Congleton (2006)`**, in Congleton and Swedenborg's *Democratic
Constitutional Design and Public Policy*, construct indices of the number of central-government
veto players required to secure a constitutional amendment. That is precisely the entrenchment
parameter κ(g) defined in the Entrenchment–Evolvability Tradeoff proposition as "the number of
veto gates required to amend constitution g." One citation, one easy objection foreclosed.

## 5. One question back

**What became of the Constitutional Persistence prediction?** Machine one can find no
"constitutional persistence" or "protection bits predict persistence time" prediction in v4; the
surviving empirical predictions are sector tipping, enterprise-driven concentration, the
cooperation/institutional-quality threshold, and barbell bimodality. If it was cut, good, and this
is moot. If it moved or was rephrased, it needs attention: the established comparative
constitutional literature runs *against* it. Lutz (1994) finds increasing amendment difficulty an
inefficient way to reduce amendments, with moderate amendment rates associated with longevity;
Elkins, Ginsburg and Melton (2009), over 935 national constitutions, find endurance promoted by
flexibility. Ginsburg and Melton (2015) report that institutional variables are never
statistically significant and often carry the wrong sign, with Tsebelis arguing the contrary.

The repair, if the prediction survives, is a distinction rather than a retreat: protection bits
measure resistance to *capture*; that literature measures resistance to *replacement*. Those are
different survival concepts and separating them is itself a contribution.
